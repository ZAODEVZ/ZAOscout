# Research: Ethereum Foundation Restructure (2026-06-23)

> Source: blog.ethereum.org/2026/06/23/ef-structure. Announced today. EF reorganizes around clusters + a ~20% headcount cut.

## The headline numbers

- **54 employees let go** - roughly **20% of the workforce**.
- Announced **June 23, 2026**.
- Severance: higher of **1 month per year worked** or the local legal minimum, plus job-placement help + small transition grants for career coaching.
- Implements the EF **Mandate** (`ethereum.foundation/ef-mandate.pdf`) and the **Treasury Management Policy** (June 2025) - the framing is "fund the critical tasks ahead, don't get whipsawed by short-term market moves."

## The new structure: 5 mission clusters + 2 support clusters

> Note: the post frames the work as **five mission clusters** (the quote says "five clusters with different domains"), with **Operations** and **Management** as the supporting two. So: 5 + 2.

1. **Protocol Layer** - harden + scale the protocol while preserving censorship resistance, open-source, privacy. Focus: fork safety, post-quantum security, MEV mitigation.
2. **Access Layer** - make self-sovereignty *practical*: reading chains, transacting privately, proving identity, delegating authority, and **exiting without intermediaries**. Applies the **"zero option" principle** - a credible intermediary-free path must always exist alongside intermediated ones.
3. **User Layer** - ground EF decisions in real user needs (segments, personas, use cases, self-sovereignty impact).
4. **Community Layer** - EF's public identity + comms; explicitly **distinguishes EF's mission from "zero-sum finance crypto"**; builds ties to allied fields (open source, privacy research, civil liberties).
5. **Institutional Layer** - engage financial institutions, enterprises, governments, nonprofits to show Ethereum integration while maximizing user sovereignty + CROPS properties.
6. **Operations Cluster** - infra + support functions.
7. **Management Cluster** - executive leadership + admin.

## What's changing vs before

- From a flatter org to **accountable clusters**, each with its own domain and its own results to answer for ("each requiring a different approach... held accountable for different results").
- A clear split between **mission work** (Protocol/Access/User/Community/Institutional) and **support** (Ops/Management).
- A deliberate narrative move: EF positioning itself *against* "zero-sum finance crypto" and *toward* self-sovereignty, privacy, civil liberties.

## Loop iteration (2026-06-23) - the bigger picture: this is the culmination of a year-long exodus

The cluster restructure post does not stand alone - it lands at the end of a turbulent 2026 for the EF:

- **Leadership exodus.** Tomasz Stańczak stepped down as co-ED (end Feb 2026). **Hsiao-Wei Wang resigned as co-ED + board member on June 22** - literally the day before this structure post. **Bastian Aue** (interim co-ED) is now the lead executive driving the restructuring. Protocol coordinators **Tim Beiko** and **Barnabé Monnot** also stepped back.
- **Scale:** ~**19 layoffs/departures in 2026**, including **8 senior figures in 5 months** and **8 researchers** quitting (5 in May alone). Core developer count reportedly fell **225 -> 169**. One outlet flags a looming **~$30M funding hole**.
- **The strategy: "Lean Ethereum" + "smaller ship."** Vitalik publicly said the EF will be a **"smaller ship," sell less ETH**, and that **his own influence will decline** - reframing the EF as a **steward of the network, not its owner**, with a broader board and decentralized decision-making. The cluster structure + 20% cut is the operational form of that pivot. The **CROPS** framework (referenced in the structure post as "CROPS properties") is the banner. *(CROPS acronym not expanded in the post - flag for next loop.)*
- **Criticism / dissent (important - don't write false consensus):** ex-researcher **Dankrad Feist** argues the departures reflect **management problems, not strategy disagreement** - noting those leaving are *supporters* of the EF's stated values. Persistent community questions about governance transparency, slow protocol decisions, and lack of long-term direction. So the "focus and decentralize" narrative is contested; some read it as distress, not discipline.

**Why this matters for the cross-link to Initium's Motus Graph:** the EF is a near-perfect live case study of an **enactment gap** - stated values (decentralization, credible neutrality, steward-not-owner) vs the revealed reality of a centralized reorg, a single interim leader, and insiders saying it's management not mission. ## Loop iteration (2026-06-23) - CROPS decoded + treasury numbers

- **CROPS expanded** (the framework behind the whole pivot): **C**ensorship-resistance, **C**apture-resistance (added later by Vitalik), **O**penness / open-source, **P**rivacy, **S**ecurity. Formalized in the **EF Mandate** (released March 13, 2026). Vitalik's thesis: Ethereum should **stop competing on speed** and be "deeply impressive" on these **cypherpunk properties**. The new "Access Layer" and "zero option" principle in today's structure post are CROPS made operational.
  - **For your CEF/composability article:** CROPS - especially censorship-resistance + openness + the "zero option" - is the most authoritative possible backing for "build permissionlessly." You can quote the EF Mandate directly.
- **Treasury (context for the 20% cut):** per Arkham (~Apr 3, 2026) the EF held ~**$270.9M** total, dominated by ~**102,400 ETH** (~$210.9M); it completed a **70,000 ETH staking target** (~$143M staked) and holds 100k+ ETH besides. That is **down sharply from ~$970.2M in Oct 2024** ($788.7M crypto + $181.5M non-crypto). EF treasury ~**0.26% of total ETH supply**. So the headcount cut + "sell less ETH" + "smaller ship" framing reads partly as **treasury preservation** after a big drawdown - not purely philosophical.

## Loop iteration (2026-06-23) - the Mandate, the upgrade delays, the funding gap, the split

- **The 38-page Mandate (March 2026) is the root cause, not just context.** It reframes the EF from **"primary guardian" -> "one of many guardians,"** deliberately reducing its own central influence. Multiple sources say the **departures are linked to Mandate disagreements**: members were reportedly asked to sign off on it, and those unwilling/unable to embrace the new direction left. *(This partly contradicts Dankrad Feist's "management not strategy" read - flag the contradiction: some say strategy/Mandate disagreement, some say management. Both are claimed; unresolved.)*
- **Roadmap fallout:** the **Glamsterdam upgrade is postponed to Q3 2026**; Vitalik is pushing the **Hegotá privacy upgrade** (privacy = the P in CROPS). So the reorg has real schedule cost.
- **The $30M number, sourced:** it's the annual sum **Trent Van Epps** (former EF core-dev coordinator) estimates is needed to sustain Ethereum's **10+ client teams** + researchers + coordination. In a ~**June 18, 2026** warning he said core dev could hit a **"slow-burning" funding crisis in 3-9 months**. So "funding hole" = a core-dev-sustainability concern, not EF insolvency.
- **The community is split (don't write false consensus):** supporters (incl. **Joe Lubin**, who says the cuts "aren't a crisis" and keep the EF focused on core tech + neutrality over commercialization) read it as a necessary correction after years of underperformance; critics fear a **rival power center fragments governance** and that losing senior researchers slows execution. Genuine disagreement, not settled.

## Loop iteration (2026-06-23, later) - market reaction, opex target, the Ethlabs spinout

- **Market reacted negatively: ETH fell ~7%** on the announcement. The "focus/discipline" narrative did not reassure traders short-term.
- **Concrete sustainability target:** the lean model aims for a long-term **~5% opex baseline** (annual spend as a share of treasury) - i.e. the cut is explicitly about spending a smaller, sustainable slice.
- **The exodus is now an org: Ethlabs.** Five former senior EF researchers launched **Ethlabs**, an independent nonprofit backed by **Joe Lubin** (+ Bitmine, SharpLink) to accelerate institutional adoption. So the talent leaving isn't just dispersing - it's reconstituting into a parallel, more-decentralized ecosystem org. (Reinforces Vitalik's "EF as one of many guardians" framing - intentionally or not, that's happening.)
- **Official cluster descriptions** (fuller than the first read): Protocol = harden/scale + "scaling self-sovereignty"; Access = self-sovereignty "available, legible and survivable" across read/transact/prove/delegate/exit; User = ground EF in real users (segments, personas, failure modes, tradeoffs); Community = make legible "what the EF stands for, and how that materially differs from zero-sum financial crypto, from corpo-compromised crypto, and from the bland, status-quo-preserving... grant-managing parts of the non-profit world"; Institutional = enterprises/governments/universities/nonprofits/financial institutions, preserving self-sovereignty + CROPS.
- Date refinement: Hsiao-Wei Wang's co-ED/board resignation is reported as **June 19** by some outlets (others said ~June 22) - minor source variance, flag as ~June 19-22.
